*Undergraduate thesis · Neotropical Primate Conservation & Lab of Socio-ecological Systems (UPCH) · Amazonas, Peru · 2025–present*

The yellow-tailed woolly monkey (*Lagothrix flavicauda*) and the Andean night monkey (*Aotus miconax*) are endemic to the montane forests of northern Peru and are both threatened. My thesis asks how these two primates, one diurnal and one nocturnal, contribute to forest regeneration through seed dispersal.

**What I did**

- Designed and implemented the field study, with a first field season in 2025 and a second planned for January 2027.
- Followed groups through diurnal and nocturnal tracking, focal observations and feeding records.
- Collected faecal samples to recover dispersed seeds and set up germination trials in the field.
- Coordinated fieldwork logistics and data management with local and international collaborators.

**Presented at:** 6th Undergraduate Scientific Congress, UPCH, Lima (2025) — *Patterns and rates of seed dispersal by* Lagothrix flavicauda *and* Aotus miconax *in a montane forest of Amazonas, Peru.*
